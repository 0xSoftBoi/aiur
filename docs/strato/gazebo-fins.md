# Gazebo cross-check of the fin sizing

An independent check of the yaw/pitch stability model in
[`flight6dof.py`](../../aiur/strato/flight6dof.py): the S2 hull with fins,
flown open loop in Gazebo Harmonic on the stock `LighterThanAirDynamics`,
`LiftDrag` and `Buoyancy` systems, against the in-repo 6-DoF from the same
initial state and thrust.

This compares two models. Nothing here is a measurement.

## Result

- **The 6-DoF's dynamics are confirmed.** With drag switched off, its
  rigid-body, added-mass, Munk and pendulum terms reproduce Gazebo's physics
  engine to within 1 % (yaw after 4 s of divergence: −33.09° against −33.43°).
- **Like for like, Gazebo agrees with the 6-DoF and is 7–15 % more
  pessimistic.** Given the same hull aerodynamics the 6-DoF assumes, the
  finned hull diverges in yaw at all three fin ratios in both models; Gazebo
  reaches 10° of yaw in 4.16 / 6.96 / 11.18 s against 4.48 / 7.76 / 13.18 s.
  The whole gap is two simplifications in the Gazebo hull plugin, not the fins.
- **With the hull aerodynamics the plugin was written for (Li & Nahon), fins
  at 0.50 and 0.60 hold heading open loop; 0.30 does not.** The 6-DoF applies
  the full potential-flow Munk moment; Li & Nahon's formulation keeps 75 % of
  it on this hull. That one term decides the answer, and it is unmeasured.
- **The existing generator had the viscous hull moment with the wrong sign**,
  and four smaller errors (below). None affect the rail check in
  [`ros.md`](ros.md).
- No error was found in the 6-DoF.

Two things stock `gz sim` cannot do, which is why there is a runner:

- **Start a model moving.** `Link::SetLinearVelocity` leaves a command
  component that the Physics system zeroes and re-applies on every later step
  (`Physics.cc` l.4169), so the body stops dead. The runner applies a one-step
  impulse from rest instead: force `(m + A)·v/dt` and torque `m·r_g × v/dt` at
  the link origin. It lands on the commanded velocity to four digits with no
  rotation, which also confirms that wrenches act at the link origin
  (`Link.cc` l.458) and that the engine carries the added mass.
- **Push along the body axis.** `ApplyLinkWrench` passes its persistent force
  straight to `AddWorldWrench` (`ApplyLinkWrench.cc` l.289, 314): it is fixed
  in the world. The runner rotates the thrust into the body frame every step.

The package's `CMakeLists.txt` installs only `gz_drag_check.py`. It was not
edited; the launch file finds the new scripts in the source tree next to the
install tree, or in `$AIUR_DESCRIPTION_SCRIPTS`. Adding them to that
`install(PROGRAMS ...)` line would remove the fallback.

## 1. What the plugins actually do

Line numbers are for the `gz-sim8` branch (8.15.0 is installed).

### LighterThanAirDynamics

For the body-frame velocity at one point, `eps_v` metres **aft** of the link
origin (`LighterThanAirDynamics.cc` l.382), with incidence
γ = atan2(crossflow, axial) ≥ 0, dynamic pressure q at that point, and n the
unit crossflow direction (l.396–420):

```
F = q (−force_inviscid·sin 2γ + force_viscous·sin² γ) · (−n)
M = q (−moment_inviscid·sin 2γ + moment_viscous·sin² γ) · (x̂ × n)
```

A positive bracket in F is a force opposing the crossflow. A positive bracket
in M turns the nose **away** from the velocity (destabilising).

The header (`LighterThanAirDynamics.hh` l.43–58) defines all four
coefficients as integrals over the separated afterbody, from ε_v to L, with
lever arm (ε_m − ε), ε_m being the centre of volume, citing Li & Nahon 2007
eq. 11 and 14:

| Parameter | Definition | Sign | Meaning |
| --- | --- | :---: | --- |
| `force_inviscid_coeff` | (k2−k1)·∫ dS | − | Potential-flow load missing from the afterbody: lift survives on the nose |
| `force_viscous_coeff` | C_dc·∫ 2R dε | + | Crossflow drag |
| `moment_inviscid_coeff` | (k2−k1)·∫ dS·(ε_m−ε) | + | **Reduces** the Munk moment |
| `moment_viscous_coeff` | C_dc·∫ 2R·(ε_m−ε) dε | − | Afterbody drag trails: stabilising |

Upstream's own test world (`test/worlds/lighter_than_air_dynamics.sdf`
l.89–92) carries exactly these signs: −, +, +, −. The Li & Nahon paper itself
was not obtained; the reading rests on the plugin source, its header, that
test world, and the runs below.

Three things the source does that the parameter names do not say:

- **The Munk moment is not in the plugin.** `AddedMassTorque` multiplies the
  `v × (A₁₁v)` term by zero (l.213). It comes from the physics engine: DART is
  given the added mass as part of the link's spatial inertia
  (gz-physics `AddedMassFeatures.cc` l.67), and its bias force then contains
  the Munk moment. `moment_inviscid_coeff` only subtracts from it.
- **The plugin repeats the other added-mass Coriolis terms.** It applies
  −ω × (A₁₁v) and −ω × (A₂₂ω) (l.192–217, 423–433), which DART has already
  applied for the same reason. They are counted twice.
- **Axial drag** is −q·C·cos²α with q on the total speed and α the
  pitch-plane incidence only (l.436–441). It ignores sideslip and always
  points along −x, also when flying backwards.

It uses the link's velocity over the ground; there is no wind.

### Measured, bare hull

Free hull, 6 m/s, 2° of sideslip, no thrust. All five diverge: the Munk
moment is destabilising in every configuration.

| --- | ---: | --- |
| No LTA plugin, `<fluid_added_mass>` only | 2.66 s | away from the flow |

| Coefficients as above, ε_v by Hopkins' rule (`li-nahon`) | 3.84 s | away |

### Were the generated coefficients right?

S2, m³ or m² as appropriate. The full Munk coefficient (k2−k1)·V is 137.96 m³.

| | As generated | Same ε_v, corrected | Hopkins ε_v (`li-nahon`) | `equivalent` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Separation station ε_v | 0.400 L | 0.400 L | 0.833 L | — |
| `force_inviscid_coeff` | −11.55 | −11.55 | −4.123 | 0 |
| `force_viscous_coeff` | 33.36 | 33.35 | 4.763 | 60.16 |
| `moment_inviscid_coeff` | 72.08 | 65.88 | 34.76 | 0 |
| `moment_viscous_coeff` | **+124.71** | −124.62 | −39.14 | −26.14 |
| `eps_v` | −0.541 | −0.541 | 7.085 | 0 |
| Munk moment kept | 48 % | 52 % | 75 % | 100 % |

- Both force coefficients: right.
- `moment_viscous_coeff`: **wrong sign.** The afterbody's crossflow drag
  pushes the stern upwind.
- `moment_inviscid_coeff`: right sign, wrong integral. It was taken over the
  nose [0, ε_v] instead of the afterbody [ε_v, L]; the two happen to be close
  at 0.4 L.
- ε_v = 0.4 L is not where Hopkins' rule puts it. The rule Li & Nahon use
  (NACA RM A51C14 eq. 4: x₀ = 0.378 L + 0.527 x₁, with x₁ the station of
  steepest fall in section area — the constants were confirmed by a search of the report's
  text, the definition of x₁ is from memory) gives 0.833 L for this
  profile. At 0.4 L half the Munk moment is discarded.

The bare hull "as generated" still diverges, more slowly than it should: too
much Munk moment removed, partly offset by the viscous moment pushing the
wrong way.

Other errors in the existing `sdf()`, none of which the rail check can see:

- `Airframe.inertia` is about the centre of volume, but it is written as the
  inertia about a centre of mass 1.21 m lower, so m·z_g² = 23.2 kg·m² is
  counted twice: I_xx is 46.33 instead of 23.10, I_yy 289.67 instead of 266.45.
- The `_float` world's thrust is world-fixed (`ApplyLinkWrench`, above). Off
  the rail it stops pointing along the hull as soon as the hull turns.

`sdf()` is unchanged. The corrected values are in `lta_coefficients()` and
`sdf_free_model()` in the new section of the generator, and only the new
models use them.

## 2. Fins on LiftDrag

Four `LiftDrag` instances on `base_link`, in a + layout: top and bottom
surfaces lifting in y, port and starboard in z. `LiftDrag` reads the velocity
and applies its wrench at a `cp` given in the link frame (`LiftDrag.cc`
l.293–296, 506–508), so no fin links or joints are needed, and four surfaces
on the hull link are exact where four welded links would add mass and
inertia. The 6-DoF lumps one fin pair per plane on the hull axis; two
surfaces of half the pair's area at the same station give the same force and
moment in that plane.

S2 at 20 km, fin ratio 0.50:

| LiftDrag parameter | Value | From the 6-DoF |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `area` | 3.9447 m², × 4 | `fin_area_total_m2 / 4`; two per plane make the pair area S_total/2 = 7.889 m² |
| `cla` | 2.2 /rad | `fin_lift_slope`, unchanged |
| `a0`, `cma`, `cma_stall` | 0 | No camber, no fin pitching moment |
| `alpha_stall` | 0.2618 rad | The 15° limit of the linear range |
| `cla_stall` | 0 | Lift holds at its 15° value, as the 6-DoF's linear term does |
| `cda` | 0 | The 6-DoF has no fin drag term; fin drag is inside `drag_multiplier` |
| `cda_stall` | 0.9416 /rad | Chosen so that broadside drag equals the 6-DoF's broadside normal force: 2.2·tan 15° + 1.2·(1 − tan 15°)² = 1.233 |
| `cp` | (−7.560, 0, ±2.862) and (−7.560, ±2.862, 0) m | x = x_cv − 0.86 L; ±1.3 R is the 6-DoF's roll-damping arm |
| `forward` | 1 0 0 | Hull axis |
| `upward` | 0 1 0 (top, bottom); 0 0 1 (port, starboard) | The plane each pair acts in |
| `air_density` | 0.08891 kg/m³ | ISA at 20 km |

Where the two fin models differ in kind:

| | 6-DoF | LiftDrag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Force in the linear range | Normal to the hull axis, ∝ sin α | Lift normal to the local velocity, ∝ α in radians (l.371, 418) |
| Past 15° | Linear term frozen, plus 1.2 × (excess crossflow)², normal to the axis | Lift frozen; drag grows linearly in α, along the velocity (l.401–448) |
| Flying backwards | Fins still act | No force at all when `forward·v ≤ 0` (l.312–316) |
| Out-of-plane flow | Ignored | Lift scaled by cos² of the sweep angle (l.337–343) |
| Velocity | Relative to the air | Link velocity over the ground, plus ω × cp; wind only if a Wind entity exists |
| Roll damping | Quadratic in roll rate | Linear, from the off-axis `cp` |
| Timing | Inside the RK4 step | From the previous step's velocity, 10 ms late |
| Hull carry-over, hull wake, downwash | Folded into 2.2 | Folded into 2.2 |

The last row is the limit of this check: both models take the same lift
slope, so Gazebo says nothing about whether 2.2 is right.

## 3. Comparison

S2, 20 km, 6 m/s, 2° of initial incidence. Two Gazebo hull models:

- `equivalent` — the aerodynamics the 6-DoF assumes: full Munk moment, no
  nose lift, crossflow drag over the whole length. It tests the fins and the
  dynamics.
- `li-nahon` — the plugin used as its source intends, ε_v by Hopkins' rule.
  It tests the 6-DoF's hull assumptions.

### (a) Yaw, coasting

Time to 10° of yaw. The 6-DoF's `handling_report` gives 5, 8 and 14 s: it
samples once a second and reports the first sample past 10°. At 10 ms the
same runs cross at 4.48, 7.76 and 13.18 s.

| Fins / V^(2/3) | 6-DoF | Gazebo `equivalent` | Ratio | Gazebo `li-nahon` |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0.30 | 4.48 s, diverges | 4.16 s, diverges | 0.93 | 8.56 s, diverges |
| 0.50 | 7.76 s, diverges | 6.96 s, diverges | 0.90 | never: settles 5.56° off |
| 0.60 | 13.18 s, diverges | 11.18 s, diverges | 0.85 | never: settles 1.84° off |

Where it settles, the sideslip is gone and the heading has stopped moving
(0.1° or less in the last 10 s). None of the cases weathercocks back to the
original heading: a stable hull turns the flight path, not the nose.

### (b) Pitch, coasting

Nothing reaches 10° in either model. The hull pitches up, slows, and the
pendulum brings it back to 0.14–0.15° by 60 s.

| Fins / V^(2/3) | 6-DoF peak | Gazebo `equivalent` peak | Gazebo `li-nahon` peak |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.30 | 7.17° | 8.60° | 3.11° |
| 0.50 | 3.41° | 3.70° | 1.59° |
| 0.60 | 2.46° | 2.61° | 1.15° |

Hull and fins are the same in pitch as in yaw; the difference is the centre
of gravity 1.214 m below the centre of volume, worth m·g·z_g = 188 N·m/rad at
any speed against a Munk moment of 442 N·m/rad at 6 m/s that falls with the
square of the speed. The 6-DoF also returns to level under cruise thrust, at
a constant 6.16 m/s, from peaks of 6.16°, 2.38° and 1.54°; that powered case
was not run in Gazebo.

Airspeed after 60 s of coasting is 2.59 m/s in the 6-DoF and 2.48 m/s in
Gazebo: the plugin's drag coefficient is fixed at its 6 m/s value, the 6-DoF's
falls with Reynolds number through the transition band.

### (c) Cruise thrust, 2.565 N along the hull axis, 240 s

| Fins / V^(2/3) | 6-DoF | Gazebo `equivalent` | Gazebo `li-nahon` |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.30 | 10° at 4.38 s | 10° at 4.08 s | 10° at 8.36 s |
| 0.50 | 10° at 7.57 s | 10° at 6.80 s | holds: 4.46° off, 6.178 m/s |
| 0.60 | 10° at 13.01 s | 10° at 11.02 s | holds: 1.59° off, 6.178 m/s |

From a perfectly aligned start the design-point model (`<slug>_fins`) holds
0.00° of heading and 0.004° of pitch, loses 0.16 m of height in 240 s, and
settles at 6.178 m/s. The 6-DoF settles at 6.161 m/s; the sizing model's
figure is 6.00.

Open loop, the 6-DoF never holds heading at any of the three ratios. That is
its design premise, not a defect: the fins were sized so that the stern
gimbal can hold it.

### Where the differences come from

`gz_fins_diagnose.py` flies the 6-DoF with Gazebo's terms substituted one at
a time. With all of them in, it reproduces Gazebo to 0.04 s, which is the
evidence that the source was read correctly. Time to 10° of yaw, coasting:

| | 0.30 | 0.50 | 0.60 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 6-DoF as written | 4.48 | 7.76 | 13.18 |
| … with the plugin's repeated added-mass Coriolis terms | 4.41 | 7.51 | 12.31 |
| … with the plugin's axial drag | 4.48 | 7.77 | 13.18 |
| … with hull crossflow at one point instead of 20 strips | 4.24 | 7.22 | 12.00 |
| … with the fins by LiftDrag | 4.46 | 7.73 | 13.13 |
| All four: emulated `equivalent` | 4.16 | 6.96 | 11.21 |
| **Gazebo `equivalent`** | 4.15 | 6.95 | 11.17 |
| `equivalent` + `moment_inviscid_coeff` (Munk reduced to 75 %) | 5.58 | 14.75 | never |
| `equivalent` + `force_inviscid_coeff` (nose lift) | 4.38 | 7.86 | 14.97 |
| `equivalent` with viscous terms on the afterbody only | 4.18 | 6.95 | 11.01 |
| All: emulated `li-nahon` | 8.57 | never | never |
| **Gazebo `li-nahon`** | 8.55 | never | never |

(Gazebo rows here are from the simulator's own state at 100 Hz; the tables
above are from bridged odometry at 50 Hz and differ by up to 0.01 s.)

- **The fin models agree to 0.5 %.** Swapping only the fins moves the result
  by 0.02–0.05 s. Every difference in kind listed in section 2 is real and
  none matters below stall.
- **`equivalent` is faster than the 6-DoF for two reasons, both in the hull
  plugin.** It evaluates crossflow at a single point, so the hull has no
  yaw-rate damping, where the 6-DoF's strips do (0.24 / 0.54 / 1.18 s). And it
  repeats added-mass terms the engine already applied (0.07 / 0.25 / 0.87 s).
  The first is a coarser model than the 6-DoF's; the second is a defect.
- **`li-nahon` differs from the 6-DoF because of one term**, the reduction of
  the Munk moment, helped by nose lift. Pitch behaves the same way: the peak
  at 0.50 goes from 3.69° to 2.13° with the Munk reduction alone, and to 1.58°
  with everything (Gazebo: 1.59°).

How much of the Munk moment survives depends on where the flow is taken to
separate. Emulated `li-nahon`, yaw, coasting:

| ε_v / L | Munk moment kept | 0.30 | 0.50 | 0.60 |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.70 | 60 % | 21.81 s | settles, 1.16° | settles, 0.17° |
| 0.80 | 70 % | 10.58 s | settles, 3.36° | settles, 1.13° |
| 0.833 (Hopkins) | 75 % | 8.57 s | settles, 5.56° | settles, 1.85° |
| 0.88 | 82 % | 6.59 s | 25.63 s | settles, 3.88° |
| 0.92 | 88 % | 5.40 s | 12.31 s | settles, 8.86° |
| 0.96 | 95 % | 4.57 s | 8.36 s | 16.04 s |

Fins at 0.50 hold heading open loop only if no more than about 80 % of the
potential-flow Munk moment is real. Hopkins' rule says 75 %. That is a thin
margin on an empirical rule fitted to wind-tunnel bodies at much higher
Reynolds number than this hull's 6.6 × 10⁵.

## 4. What this does and does not validate

Validated, in the sense of two independent implementations agreeing:

- The 6-DoF's equations of motion: rigid body with an offset centre of
  gravity, added mass, the Munk moment's sign and size, the pendulum.
- Its fin force as a function of incidence and rate, below stall.
- The ordering of the three fin ratios, and that pitch is safe where yaw is
  not.

Not validated:

- **The fin lift slope.** Both models use 2.2 /rad. It is still an estimate.
- **How much of the Munk moment the real hull feels.** The two Gazebo hull
  models bracket it, and the answer to "does 0.50 hold heading" flips between
  them.
- **Anything closed loop.** The 0.50 choice rests on gimbal saturation in a
  90° turn (design log, Rev H). Gazebo flew no controller and no gimbal.
- Stall, fin–hull interference, gusts, film fins that deform.

**Verdict on fin ratio 0.50: supported, not validated.** Where Gazebo shares
the 6-DoF's hull assumptions it reproduces its numbers, a little worse and
for understood reasons. Where it does not, it says the 6-DoF is conservative:
the hull is less unstable than assumed and 0.50 is stable outright. Nothing
found argues for larger fins. Nothing found would justify smaller ones
either: at 0.30 the hull diverges in every model.

To call the sizing validated would take, in rough order of value:

1. A measured lift slope for a fin pair on this hull at the fin's Reynolds
   number: a tow test or wind-tunnel model, or a rate-response identification
   on the SP1 trainer.
2. A measured hull yawing moment against incidence, which fixes the Munk
   fraction and ε_v. A captive hull on a yaw pivot in a steady stream does it.
3. An open-loop sideslip release on a flying trainer, compared with the
   tables above. Divergence in about 7 s points at the 6-DoF's hull; settling
   points at Li & Nahon's.
4. The gimbal loop flown in Gazebo on the `li-nahon` hull, to repeat the
   Rev H turn study against the second hull model.
